🏁 Australian Grand Prix
📝 League Recap

Welcome to the new era. The 2026 Formula 5 season opened with the Australian Grand Prix under a completely overhauled ruleset — two-player teams, head-to-head matchups, the BOX BOX over/under line, and a real team championship that matters. With 48 players across 24 teams split into Championship Division and Second Division, nobody quite knows what strategies will work yet. Which drivers are worth the top pick? How aggressive should your needle guess be? Does the Over/Under matter more than raw driver points? Round 1 gave us some early answers — and raised a lot more questions.

George Russell won the real Australian Grand Prix for Mercedes, but the biggest winner in the F5 universe was his teammate Kimi Antonelli. Players who picked the Mercedes rookie averaged 41.2 pts compared to 22.5 pts for the six who didn't. All six Antonelli-skippers finished in the bottom eight.

And then there's the elephant in the room: a bot won. The Formula5 Bot — Claude AI, picking on behalf of TJ Premium — took the top individual spot with 61 points, nailing the needle at 2.2s and picking Oliver Bearman when most humans didn't. All 48 of us spent the week agonizing over our lineups, and a large language model casually walked in and beat every single one of us. Let's see what happens next week. One point behind the machine, reigning champion Andrew Ishak posted 60 in his first week back after Cal Aggie Racing's relegation to Second Division. And Mena Yousef, a two-time top-5 finisher in the Players Championship, rounded out the human podium at 55.

The real story of Round 1, though, was the BOX BOX carnage. Ferrari's first pit stop came in at just 2.22 seconds — well under most players' guesses (the league average was 2.64s). That meant UNDER won 11 out of 12 matchups, and five matchups were outright decided by the BOX BOX swing — meaning if the pit stop had gone the other way, the losing team would have won instead. On the needle side, Ronnie Nobar, Kevin Coolidge, and the F5 Bot all guessed 2.2s for a perfect +5. Meanwhile, Heather Ishak, Maggie Mudge, Brian Dong, and Chris Fondacaro scored zero needle points AND were on teams that lost the over/under.

Nobody — not a single player out of 48 — managed to predict all five drivers in the correct finishing order. The order bonus remains the white whale. Best finish was more attainable: 18 players correctly guessed P2 and picked up the +3 bonus, while 25 guessed P1 and missed. Andrew Ishak and Kevin Coolidge — the last two Players Champions, now teamed up on the relegated Cal Aggie Racing — both posted top-13 individual scores and won their matchup despite the BOX BOX headwind. On the other end, Aggie Slipstream — piloted by Andrew's former roommates Brian Dong and Dan Patry — lived up to their reputation with the lowest team score of the week at 49.
