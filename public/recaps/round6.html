Really, Cadillac?

Round 6 -- Monaco Grand Prix
Monaco Recap

Cadillac Murders the Under

The pit question this week was Cadillac's first pit stop, and Cadillac, in their debut season, decided to answer it with a 4.5-second stop. Four and a half seconds. On the first lap. I have been staring at that number for two days.

Really, Cadillac? You couldn't even TRY to have a decent pitstop? And on the first lap? Just killing the hopes of the under before the race got underway.

Here is what 4.5 did to Formula 5: it sat above every single BOX BOX line in the entire league. All 24 of them. Which means the OVER won every matchup it was assigned to, BOX BOX went a perfect 12-0 in favor of OVER, and all twelve UNDER teams took the -1 before their drivers had even settled into a rhythm. The under was not beaten. The under was dead on arrival, run over by a Cadillac doing a four-and-a-half-second pit stop. And it gets better: not one of the 48 of us scored a single individual needle point either. The closest guesses in the whole league were Scott Schertler at 4.0 and Paul Kohli at 3.7, and even those were nowhere near. Nobody on Earth saw 4.5 coming, because 4.5 is not a pit stop, it is a cry for help.

Rafik Takes the Week and Van City Takes the Division

Rafik Zarifa won Round 6 with 60 points, the highest single-week score in the league this season. He was one of only four players who picked Lewis Hamilton for the top driver slot (+18), and he paired it with 32 midfield points, the best midfield total of the entire field. While the rest of us were getting -1 and arguing about a Cadillac, Rafik was quietly putting up a 60 in Monaco.

And it mattered. Van City Corsa beat East Bay Racing 81-71 and vaulted to the top of the Championship Division at 73 points, knocking XLIX Racing Team off the perch they have held all season. Rafik built this team around marrying into Vancouver and a love of the word Corsa, and now it sits first in the division. His teammate Mena Yousef added 32. The vibes in Vancouver, which had soured in Round 5, are back.

The Leclerc Trap (Again We Fall For It)

The top driver pool was Leclerc, Hamilton, and Norris. Antonelli won the race, of course, but he was not an option, so it did not matter. What mattered is that 33 of us picked Charles Leclerc, who promptly crashed under the safety car, and 11 of us picked Lando Norris, who DNF'd. That is 44 of 48 players getting -1 on the top pick. Only the four Hamilton pickers escaped: Rafik Zarifa, Maggie Mudge, Danny Bowers, and Zack Girgis, who all banked +18 while the rest of us banked regret.

This is the second week in a row the popular pick has detonated. In Montreal it was Russell and Norris. In Monaco it was Leclerc and Norris. The lesson, apparently, is that picking the obvious driver in Formula 5 is a trap, and the lesson, apparently, is one I personally refuse to learn, because I picked Leclerc too.

Two Dead-Even Ties, Decided by a Cadillac

Because the top pick was a bloodbath and almost everyone landed in the same midfield range, the base scores this week were stacked on top of each other, and that put the BOX BOX line in charge of the outcome. Two matchups finished in an exact 63-63 tie on base before BOX BOX, and the Cadillac stop snapped both of them. Drivetex and Garra Dynamics were knotted at 63 apiece until Drivetex's OVER +5 and Garra's UNDER -1 handed it to Drivetex 68-62. Meatballs and Cal Aggie Racing were also tied at 63, and the same swing gave Meatballs the win 68-62.

I would like to note, for the record and with great personal pain, that Cal Aggie Racing is my team. We tied Meatballs to the point on base, then lost on a pit stop neither of us could have predicted. Two more matchups flipped on the line as well: Wildcat Motors beat Stalloni 1851 66-64 after trailing 61-65 on base, and Scuderia Iskandaraya beat Magic Kingdom Racing 51-49 after trailing 46-50. Four matchups decided by a number nobody guessed. Beautiful sport.

We Are No Longer All Losing to the Bot

A historic development. After six rounds, three humans have finally climbed above the Formula5 Bot in the cumulative individual standings: Francisco Soldavini leads everyone at 240, Joe McGlynn is at 232, and Scott Schertler is at 231. The Bot has slipped to 4th at 230, tied with me. The machines are, for now, contained.

The cruel irony: the two humans at the very top both watched their teams lose on the BOX BOX line this week. Soldavini's Garra lost the 63-63 tie to Drivetex, and Schertler's Stalloni got flipped by Wildcat. You can be the best player in the league and still go home because a Cadillac mechanic had a rough Sunday. That is the whole game in one sentence.
