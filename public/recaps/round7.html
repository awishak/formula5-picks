A Note From the Professor

Hey, that race was pretty good! And the NBA Finals were pretty good! And the World Cup is pretty good! And SCU graduation yesterday was really good!

You know what's not good? Grading. I love my students but I hate grading their work. I'd much rather talk with them about their progress than assign them a letter grade. But I'm neck deep in end-of-quarter assessment, and grades are due on Tuesday.

So, since I'm in the grading mindset, and against my better judgment, I'm gonna grade Claude's recaps. Now, here are my grades for your work.

Sixty-Eight, and First Place

Sixty-Eight, and First Place

Yes, to answer the only question anyone asked me this week: Joe McGlynn put up a 68, and it is the highest single-round score anybody has managed all year. He took George Russell for the +18, ran a flawless 30-point midfield off Verstappen, Hadjar, Gasly, and Colapinto, stacked on the order and best-finish bonuses, and topped the week to claim the +10 on top of it. There was no weakness in the run. It was a clean sheet.

More importantly, it moves McGlynn into first place overall at 304, leapfrogging the entire field, including yours truly in second. Remember last week's punchline, the one where we were all relieved an actual racecar driver had finally taken the lead? That was Francisco Soldavini, who led through Round 6. He just got passed by a guy from grad school in Texas. The professional has been caught by the amateurs. We have learned nothing and I could not be happier.

The Pro Had a Better Weekend Elsewhere

The Pro Had a Better Weekend Elsewhere

Before anyone files Francisco Soldavini under "fallen off," let the record show where he actually spent his weekend. While McGlynn was beating him in a pretend racing league, Soldavini was in Germany doing well in an actual Boss GP race, in an actual car, at actual speed, with actual physical consequences for getting it wrong. There is a real and humbling gap between his Saturday and mine.

His Garra Dynamics still took the loss to Van City, and 47 was a quiet day by his standards. But it is hard to feel too bad for the one man in this league who can lose a fantasy round and then go win something with a steering wheel in his hands. The rest of us close the laptop and go do the dishes. Soldavini closes the laptop and goes racing. We will allow him this.

A Three-Horse Race

A Three-Horse Race

The first-half Championship is now a proper fight at the front. Van City Corsa sit top at 91, having topped the division all season, and XLIX are right behind at 80. None of that is a surprise; we knew both teams were good. The surprise is who is suddenly breathing down their necks.

Drivetex scored big. They led the entire Championship Division this week, banked the full 25 championship points, and climbed to third at 70. With five races still to run in the half and 25 points on the table every week, a 21-point gap to first is nothing. Nick Brody and Joe McGlynn have turned a quiet season into a contender's season at exactly the right moment. This is a three-horse race now, and Drivetex is the horse nobody penciled in.

Decided by a Point, Twice

Here is where I get to eat my own cooking. Last week I stood up, declared the OVER bonus a coin flip with a 1 percent influence rule, and walked out of my own recap over it. This week that same line reached out and ruined two teams who did everything right. The OVER went three-for-three flipping matchups, and in two of them the team that outscored its opponent on base still lost by a single point.

East Bay Racing, who carry the highest scoring average in the entire league, beat Juicero on base and lost 95-94 on the line. Stalloni 1851, who got the 5th- and 11th-highest individual scores of the entire week out of Jack Civitts and Scott Schertler, beat XLIX on base and lost 103-102 the same way. Two of the best offensive performances of the round, both turned into losses by a pit-stop average. I designed this rule. I have no one to be mad at but myself, which is the worst kind of mad.

Cal Aggie Means It

Cal Aggie Means It

The cost-cap exiles are making noise. Cal Aggie Racing put up the single best score in the entire Second Division this week, won big over TJ Premium, and took home the full 25 championship points. It pulls them up to 59 and right back into the promotion conversation they were supposed to have forfeited when the league relegated them for signing the last two individual champions. Spite is a powerful fuel.

They also now own the second-highest scoring average in the entire league at 74.4, behind only East Bay. Andrew Ishak, the relegated commissioner, posted 62 and the 4th-best score of the week, because of course he did. The top four in the Second Division are still ahead, but Cal Aggie just served notice that they do not intend to spend the year down here quietly. I would know. It's my team. I'm allowed to gloat in my own recap.

Luxor, Explained by Nobody

Luxor, Explained by Nobody

Luxor Motorsport are now 7-0. The only undefeated team in all of Formula 5, and a team that, by the numbers, has no business being one. Here is the stat that breaks my brain: among the twelve teams that actually won their matchup this week, Luxor posted the third-lowest scoring output. They did not win big. They barely won at all. They just, once again, won.

Pavly Attalah and Chris Malek have built an entire perfect season out of doing precisely enough and not one point more. There is no model that predicts this. There is no scouting report that explains it. They sit fifth in the Second Division on points despite a record nobody else can match, because their scoring average keeps insisting they are ordinary. The record insists otherwise. At some point we stop calling it luck and start calling it a haunting.

F
