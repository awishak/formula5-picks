Round 15 · Azerbaijan Grand Prix

The Azerbaijan GP in Numbers

The lowest scoring week in league history, the Azerbaijan GP stands as an outlier in this F5 season. Scores were low across the board, including the first ever negative scores, achieved by eight people. And the team matchups were just as strange: one team won their matchup by scoring exactly one point.

1

The Azerbaijan GP led to the lowest F5 scores of the season by every measure, and by a wide margin.

Through fourteen rounds the average F5 score had never dipped below 26.1, and eight of those weeks averaged over 38. Azerbaijan came in at 6.9, with half the league on 4 points or fewer. And the drop holds with the Needle and the weekly bonus stripped out: it was the drivers that sank the week, not the missing pit stop.

Eight players and one team finished the week below zero, which nobody had done before.

An F5 score is five drivers, a finishing-order bonus, a best-finish guess, the Needle and a weekly bonus for the top ten, and a driver who finishes outside the points costs the people holding him a point. These eight had Norris on top for minus one and two midfielders who finished out of the points, with nothing from the order, the best finish or the Needle to cover it. And Garra Dynamics put two of them on the same team: Ryan Kohli and Francisco Soldavini, for a team score of -6.

Lindblad was F5 driver of the week with 168 points, the lowest winning total of the season and less than half the previous low.

Every driver in the pool earns each of his pickers the same points, and adding those up across all 48 hands gives his league total for the week, which is how the driver of the week is named. Fourteen rounds in, the driver of the week had never scored the league fewer than 368, including 4 weeks over 850. And Lindblad's 168 came off 28 pickers at 6 points each: the best driver in the pool finished seventh.

Brett Dillon won the week with 30. Every other round's leader scored 48 or more, and 7 of them scored 60 or more.

And there was no pit stop.

On top of the craziness of this week, Aston Martin retired without a pit stop, meaning there were no pit stop points in either the team or the individual competition. The Needle paid nobody and BOX BOX was a push in all twelve matchups. However, a BOX BOX moves a matchup by six points, and only four of the twelve were close enough that the line could have decided them.
